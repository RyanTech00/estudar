# Your first plan

The plan is your **typical week**: which subject you study each day, for how long and with what activity. Open it from **Week → Edit plan** (or **Create plan** on the home screen).

## Three ways to start

1. **Example plan** — a complete Computer Science plan with two languages. Good for seeing how the app works.
2. **From your degree record** — if you've entered your modules in the [Degree record](/en/features/degree-record), tick **"I'm taking this module now"** and they're added to the plan with their ECTS credits and assessment dates.
3. **By hand** — add subjects in the editor.

## Fill in the essentials

| Field | What for |
|---|---|
| **Dates** (start, exams) | Semester phases and the countdown to exams |
| **Subjects**: name, short code, type, load | Type = University, Languages or Other; load = light, medium, heavy |
| **ECTS** (optional) | Basis for the time allocation (ECTS are the European credits that measure a module's workload) |
| **Exam date per subject** (optional) | Review only on the day before; priority in the final week. No date yet? The general date is used — once you know it, enter it and everything adjusts |
| **Hours per day** | The AI and the check won't let the plan go over this limit |

## Generate with AI

In **Generate with AI**, add notes if you like ("I work Tuesday afternoons", "the PF exam is written") and click **Generate weekly plan**. The AI receives the subjects, dates, hours and the **suggested time split**, and has to follow the rules described in [Weekly plan with AI](/en/features/ai-plan).

## Review before saving

The AI can make mistakes. That's why the editor runs a fixed **scientific check** — always, even on plans made by hand:

<Shot src="/screenshots/en/plano-verificacao.png" alt="Scientific check of the plan" caption="Each line is a rule. A warning doesn't stop you saving, but it tells you what to adjust." />

- Does each day's load fit within the available hours?
- Do medium and heavy subjects appear on **at least 2 days** (spacing)? A review inside another subject's session counts if it mentions the short code ("Review OS").
- Is there **retrieval practice** (self-testing, recall, mock exams, a cumulative review)?
- Are there sessions based on **rereading, highlighting or summarising**?
- Do **languages** have short, frequent sessions (3+ per week)?

You can delete sessions or add them yourself with **+ Add a session by hand**. When it looks right, click **Save plan**.

## When to change the plan

- **You found out an exam date** → enter it on the subject (or on the assessment, in the degree record). You don't need to regenerate: the alerts and priority adjust.
- **You've taken closed-book tests** → the suggested split changes with your mastery. Regenerate when the gap from the plan is large (the editor shows "in plan: X%" alongside).
- **New semester** → in the degree record, mark the new modules as "taking now"; the ones you've passed leave the plan on their own.
