# Day to day

The method comes down to four habits. The app is designed so that each one is a single tap.

## 1. Start with the next session

The **Today** tab shows **Up next** — the first session of the day not yet done — with the concrete activity ("Mixed loop exercises without looking anything up"). **Start focus** picks the subject, starts the timer and opens focus mode.

Alerts appear above it when needed:

| Alert | When |
|---|---|
| 🟥 **Tomorrow: X exam** | The day before — today only retrieval and review, no new material, and a good night's sleep |
| 🟨 **X: exam in N days** | Final week — that subject jumps to the front; shortcut to a closed-book test |
| 🟥 **X: failed in the regular period** | The resit date is missing from the degree record |

## 2. Attempt before you see the answer

During the block, work **from memory**: solve, answer, explain — and only then check. On paper, if the exam is on paper.

At the end of the block (5 minutes or more), the app asks:

1. **What did you do?** — *New material* (first exposure: it counts, but not towards mastery) or *Exercises / retrieval*.
2. **How many did you attempt?** — a blank exercise counts as attempted and wrong.
3. **How sure are you?** (1–4) — **before you mark**.
4. **Did you use AI, notes or worked examples?**
5. **Confirm and go mark** — your confidence is locked. Only now do you enter **how many you got right**.

The order is the point: a confidence rating given after seeing the answers measures nothing. See [Block log](/en/features/block-log).

## 3. One closed-book test per week, per subject

In **Progress → Closed-book test**: you choose the subject and the time (15–45 min) and do, on paper and without notes, exercises you have **not** solved in the last 7 days, mixing topics. It's the only source of your **mastery**.

- No past exams? Keep a few exercises from each problem sheet just for this.
- Starting from zero? That's normal — the test only covers the material you've already studied.

## 4. Read Progress in the right order

Progress sorts subjects like this:

1. **Overconfidence** — you expected to get more right than you did. It's the most useful signal you have.
2. **Lowest measured mastery**.
3. **No test yet**, by nearest exam date.

It also flags **relies on help** (much better with help than unaided, and help used most of the time) and **weak foundation** (a prerequisite not yet taken, failed or graded below 12 out of 20).

## What about time?

It's there — minutes per day and per subject, day streaks — but in the background. Time is what you put in; mastery is what you learn. The app won't let you confuse the two.
